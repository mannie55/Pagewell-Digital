## What we learned

- **No growth result on record yet.** Nobody has paid any of the team for SEO, ads or content. Proof so far is five live client builds in four countries: a UK tutoring company (ELI), a UK property/cleaning/education firm (DUV), a Nigerian domestic staffing agency (DSH), a Canadian cleaning company (ZLC) and a US tech professionals' association (FEDTC). There are also four concept builds (StaffOS SaaS marketing site with a full case study, Beyond Fitness studio on Next.js, Aura fragrance store and LavaX agency site on Webflow) and an unlaunched startup web app.
- **Three of our own builds have the problem we're about to sell against** (checked 2026-11-25): ZLC's `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` point at `localhost:3000`; FEDTC has no sitemap or robots.txt (both 404); DUV's sitemap lists 1 URL on the non-www host while the site runs on www. Also: DSH keeps its services as homepage anchors, ELI has no content for exam searches, FEDTC shows "©2025" and placeholder social links. Titles, meta descriptions and H1s weren't checked (the fetch tool returns text only); confirm with a crawl.
- **The buyer is an owner who already paid for a website that brings nothing.** Cheap, template or AI-built, live, invisible, untouched. Founder's words: people "just feel like they have a website."
- **The status quo, not other agencies, is the competitor.** The owner believes the job is done because the site exists. The copy has to show them it isn't, using their own numbers where possible.
- **Positioning line chosen by the founder:** "The website growth your business needs." It keeps the website as the thing they buy (provable today) and growth as the reason (the promise). "We grow your business" was rejected as a bigger promise than the team can back yet.
- **Five past clients already trust the team**, across both target markets and two more (Canada, US). That's the path to the first growth case study, and a true trust line for the site.

## The decision

Continue, narrowed. Rebuild the one-page site into a multi-page website-growth site (Approach B) **and**, in parallel, run a discounted 90-day growth pilot with past clients so proof matures alongside the site.

Rules the copy and offer follow (all 7 premises agreed):

1. Write against the dead website, not against other agencies.
2. No result claims until results exist. Sell **diagnosis** (free audit that shows the owner what they're losing), **open process** (exactly what happens each month) and a **guarantee on what we control** (deliverables and reporting). Never promise rankings.
3. Pilot with past clients (5 candidates) starts now, in exchange for publishing before/after numbers. Defects in our own builds get fixed free first, as warranty work.
4. Pagewell's own site is case study #0, tracked from launch day.
5. One build offer + 3 monthly growth tiers with a floor, priced separately in ₦ and £/$. **Prices are never published** (founder decision, 2026-11-25): the site's only job is to get the owner to contact us. Tiers are used on sales calls and in proposals.
6. Multi-page because buyers and Google both look for trust signals: who is behind the site, how to reach them, policies, one page per service, proof. Google publishes no required-pages list; its Search Quality Rater Guidelines tell raters to check who runs a site, how to contact them and what its reputation is. That's what "pages Google trusts" really means.
7. **Websites only for now** (founder decision, 2026-11-25). Software / web apps stay off the site until the startup case study is ready, then come back as a second service line, not a second headline. Client sites with app features (FEDTC's membership login, ZLC's booking flow) are shown as websites.

Ruled out: A (copy swap only), because one page can't rank for 7 services and the Google clock would start late. C (audit engine first), because the site would stay thin. C's audit lives on as the main CTA.

### Design constraints (founder direction, 2026-11-25)

Minimal, industry-standard, Apple-inspired, and it must look like the landing page that already exists.

- **The existing design system wins.** Tokens in `app/globals.css` (neutral + flamingo palette, `heading-h*` sizes, `container-page`), Instrument Sans headlines with one Instrument Serif accent word, components in `components/ui/`. New pages reuse these. Nothing off-system without flagging it.
- **Apple's principles, not Apple's assets.** Clarity (one idea per section, large confident type, short lines), deference (content first, UI recedes, flamingo used sparingly as the single accent), generous whitespace, quiet motion (the existing blur-in heading reveal, nothing louder). Keep Instrument Sans: SF Pro's licence covers mock-ups for Apple platforms, not web fonts on a public site.
- **shadcn/ui only where the repo has no component.** Likely candidates: FAQ accordion (if the current one isn't reusable), audit form inputs/select, services dropdown in the header (NavigationMenu), breadcrumbs on service and case-study pages, mobile nav (Sheet). Initialise with Tailwind v4 and map shadcn's CSS variables onto the existing scheme tokens so every component inherits the landing page's look.
- **SEO stays non-negotiable** (one h1 per page, semantic landmarks, server-rendered indexable text, no layout shift), and pages stay within the existing page-speed baseline: interactive parts stay small client islands.
